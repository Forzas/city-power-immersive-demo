# Urban Systems Education Project | Post-Sorting Waste Journey Demo Draft v1

## 1. Demo Name
The Journey After Waste Sorting: Where Does Your Waste Go?

## 2. One-Line Introduction
Waste does not disappear when it enters a bin; it simply begins another complex journey through the city.

## 3. Why Build This Demo First?
### Fundamentals
- **Strong everyday relevance:** Almost everyone has encountered waste sorting.
- **Powerful knowledge gap:** Many people know they should sort waste but do not know what happens afterward.
- **Clear branching process:** Different waste categories follow different treatment routes, making the subject ideal for interaction.
- **Visually dramatic:** Disposal, transfer, sorting, incineration, recycling, and reuse create obvious transformations.

### Assumptions
- Users care strongly about whether sorted waste is later mixed together. **Confidence: High**
- This concrete topic is more likely to be shared than an abstract environmental issue. **Confidence: High**

## 4. Intended Learning Outcomes
- Understand that sorting determines downstream treatment rather than serving as a symbolic exercise.
- Recognize that different waste categories have different destinations.
- Understand that urban cleanliness depends on both front-end sorting and back-end system coordination.

## 5. Core Interactions
### Primary Interactions
- **Choose among four waste streams:** Food / Recyclable / Hazardous / Residual.
- **Route-tracing animation:** Follow each category through its downstream process.
- **Expandable nodes:** Transfer station, sorting center, treatment facility, waste-to-energy plant, and material recovery.

### Supporting Interactions
- Incorrect-disposal correction explaining the consequences of sorting mistakes.
- City map showing how waste is transported.
- Destination comparison: incineration, landfill, recycling, and reuse.

## 6. Page Structure
### A. Opening Question
- Does the waste you throw away really “disappear”?

### B. Starting Point: Household Disposal
- Users begin at different waste bins.
- Include a small “Choose what to discard” interaction.

### C. Intermediate Process
#### 1) Sorted Collection
- Different categories enter separate containers and collection processes.

#### 2) Transfer Station
- Waste usually goes through consolidation, compaction, and transfer before final treatment.

#### 3) Sorting or Treatment Center
- Recyclables: additional sorting and reprocessing.
- Food waste: resource recovery or specialized treatment.
- Hazardous waste: dedicated safe treatment.
- Residual waste: incineration or landfill.

#### 4) Final Destinations
- Waste-to-energy incineration
- Recovered materials
- Safe treatment
- Limited landfill disposal

### D. Reinforcement Module
- Why front-end sorting matters.
- Why collection that appears mixed is not necessarily mixed treatment.
- How sorting mistakes increase processing costs.

### E. Operating Modes
#### Mode 1: Normal Sorting Day
- Each category enters the appropriate system.

#### Mode 2: High Error Rate
- Back-end sorting costs rise.
- Recyclable materials become contaminated.

#### Mode 3: Holiday Waste Peak
- Transfer and treatment systems experience higher demand.

## 7. Visual Direction
- Primary colors: Green, orange, gray, and blue for the different categories.
- Motion: Waste enters different paths from the disposal point, with colors distinguishing each route.
- Graphics: Waste-bin starting point, city map, and treatment-facility destinations.

## 8. Closing Line
What you discard is not merely waste—your choice helps determine whether it is incinerated, recycled, or transformed into a new resource.
