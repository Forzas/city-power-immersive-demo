# Urban Systems Education Project | Urban Water Supply Demo Draft v1

## 1. Demo Name
Urban Water Supply: How Does Water Reach Your Home?

## 2. One-Line Introduction
From a natural source to your faucet and onward to wastewater treatment, every drop follows an invisible journey through the city.

## 3. Why Build This Demo First?
### Fundamentals
- **Highly familiar:** Everyone uses water every day, so the topic has a low barrier to entry.
- **Naturally clear process:** Source → treatment plant → pipe network → neighborhood/home → drainage → wastewater treatment.
- **Visually effective:** Pipe networks, flow directions, cross-sections, and changing conditions are easy to illustrate.
- **Complements the power-grid demo:** Together, they establish the first collection about infrastructure supporting everyday life.

### Assumptions
- Users are naturally curious about why tap water is ready to use. **Confidence: High**
- The water system will produce clearer early feedback than a more abstract energy topic. **Confidence: Medium-high**

## 4. Intended Learning Outcomes
- Understand that tap water does not appear out of nowhere.
- Recognize purification, transmission, pressure boosting, drainage, and wastewater treatment as one continuous system.
- Develop a stronger awareness of the infrastructure behind reliable urban life.

## 5. Core Interactions
### Primary Interactions
- **End-to-end overview:** Show a drop of water traveling from nature to a home and back to the environment.
- **Clickable nodes:** Select each stage to reveal an explanation.
- **Scenario switching:** Normal day / Heavy rain / Peak demand.

### Supporting Interactions
- Building cross-section showing how water reaches upper floors.
- Pipe-network view showing the relationship between underground mains and branches.
- Wastewater animation explaining where used water goes.

## 6. Page Structure
### A. Opening Question
- How does water reach the faucet in your home?

### B. End-to-End Flow
1. Water source
2. Treatment plant
3. Transmission through trunk mains
4. Secondary pressurization and neighborhood distribution
5. Household use
6. Drainage system
7. Wastewater treatment plant
8. Return to nature or reuse

### C. Node Explanations
#### 1) Water Source
- Water comes from reservoirs, rivers, groundwater, and other sources.
- It must be treated before entering homes.

#### 2) Treatment Plant
- Coagulation, sedimentation, filtration, and disinfection.
- Key message: Drinkable water is not naturally clean; it undergoes strict treatment.

#### 3) Transmission and Distribution Network
- Trunk mains carry water to different city districts.
- Branch networks deliver it to neighborhoods and buildings.

#### 4) Secondary Water Supply
- High-rise buildings generally require additional pressure.
- Explain why supplying tall buildings is more complex than supplying low-rise ones.

#### 5) Household Use
- Cooking, bathing, toilet flushing, and laundry feed into different drainage branches.

#### 6) Wastewater Treatment
- Domestic wastewater is not discharged directly into nature.
- It must be collected, purified, and treated to meet environmental standards.

### D. Operating Modes
#### Mode 1: Normal Operation
- Stable flow, balanced pressure, and continuous supply.

#### Mode 2: Morning and Evening Peaks
- Demand rises in certain areas.
- The system must adjust distribution and pressure.

#### Mode 3: Heavy Rain
- Drainage and wastewater systems face higher pressure.
- This can introduce the drainage and flood-control topic.

## 7. Visual Direction
- Primary colors: Blue, cyan, and white.
- Motion: Water flows between nodes, with key nodes illuminated.
- Graphics: City cross-section, underground pipe network, and enlarged neighborhood detail.

## 8. Closing Line
When you turn on a faucet, an entire citywide water system has already coordinated countless steps on your behalf.
